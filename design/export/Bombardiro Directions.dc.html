My pick 1a Sixty seconds
  1. It's the app, scaled up. Same night palette, lime, centre circle and floodlight cones, so people recognise it the moment they install.
  2. One idea does two jobs. Scrolling through a turn is the creative hook and the explanation of the rules at once.
  3. It's almost free in budget terms. The H1 is the largest paint, the clock is CSS and the screens are real. The shader is a bonus, never a dependency.

1aSixty secondsThe page is one turn
Idea
A turn is a clock running down under floodlights, so the home page is that minute. A match clock sits on the centre spot and runs down as you scroll, from 01:00 to the whistle. Each section is a beat of the turn, and the real screens arrive where their own clocks say: the live card at 00:40, the red seconds at 00:07, the scoreboard at full time.
Motion
Clock and bar run on a CSS scroll timeline, with a 1 KB fallback. The last 10 seconds turn alarm red. An optional floodlight shader (about 5 KB) runs on capable devices only. The whistle blows at 00:00, with sound off by default. Reduced motion: no counting, and each beat shows its fixed time.
Trade-offs
+ The same stadium as the app: Slate night, lime, centre circle, floodlights.
+ One mechanic explains the game while you scroll.
− Long reads on dark are harder. Guides would switch to the app's light Chalk grass theme, or follow the phone the way the app does.
Try it
Scroll inside the phone, or press Play the turn. Both clocks follow.

1bEvery word is footballThe site is made of the words
Idea
Nearly 1,700 words is the product, so the site is built from them. Real words from the game roll in rows like a terrace banner, on the icon's lime. The headline is the one row that never moves, and its last line is already scored. Tap any word and it turns into a goal, the way a tap works in the app.
Motion
Rows drift at different speeds and lean into scroll speed. A visible pause control covers WCAG 2.2.2. A tap slides a black goal bar under the word (180 ms) and counts it. Reduced motion: the wall stands still, and taps still score.
Trade-offs
+ The loudest and most shareable. Every word is real text in the page's language, which helps searches for football words.
− Lime tires the eye on long reads, and marquees are a familiar jury trope. Guides would need a calmer surface.
Try it
Tap words in either mock to score them. Pause stops the rows.

1cMatchday programmePrinted matter for a game played out loud
Idea
Football nights used to come on paper: the programme, the team sheet, the ticket stub. Here the site is printed matter in the app's Chalk grass theme, with a masthead wordmark, a strict grid, the match settings set as a team sheet, and football words on perforated cards. They're the same cards the Words page prints for a game without a phone.
Motion
Almost nothing moves on load, because it's print. Cards flip on tap to show how to describe the word (420 ms). Pages turn like paper with native view transitions. Reduced motion: instant flips and crossfades only.
Trade-offs
+ The calmest and most readable. Guides and the printable Words page come almost for free.
+ Unlike any dark neon game site.
− The least party-like of the three. Dark screenshots sit on paper as plates, a step away from the app's night look.
Try it
Tap a card to flip it and see how to describe the word.
